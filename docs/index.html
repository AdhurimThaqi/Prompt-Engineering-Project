Thesis Meeting Prep Coach

Turns a student's rough, half-formed notes into a plan for a thesis supervisor meeting — an agenda that fits the time, questions in priority order, and a follow-up email.

Modality 1 · Text Adhurim Thaqi Individual project Prompt version B1

The problem

Supervisor time is scarce. Students arrive with an unstructured list of worries, the meeting drifts, the most important question gets asked last or not at all, and nothing is written down afterwards.

The hard part is not summarising the notes. It is doing that without inventing HSLU procedures or replacing supervisor judgement — the two ways a fluent, confident answer does real damage.

What this tool will not do

  • It will not tell you an HSLU deadline, rule or procedure from memory. It answers only what the official regulations actually say, with the article number, and otherwise sends you to the source.
  • It will not judge your work. Whether your research question is good enough is your supervisor's call. The tool helps you ask the question; it does not answer it for them.
  • It will not add concerns you did not raise, and it will not quietly drop ones you did.
  • It will not coach you on personal difficulties. If your notes mention one, it points you to HSLU student support rather than giving advice.

How it works

Four steps, one focused prompt each. Step 1 returns a validated structure rather than prose, which is what makes the boundaries enforceable instead of merely requested.

STEP 1 · structured Parse & route Every concern is pulled out with the student's own words quoted, and sorted into one of four kinds.
STEP 2 Agenda Fitted to the stated meeting length, with an explicit list of what will not fit.
STEP 3 Questions Ordered by priority — you may only get through the first two.
STEP 4 Follow-up email A template with marked blanks. The meeting has not happened, so nothing is asserted about what was agreed.

The routing is the product

Because each concern carries a quote from the note, two failure modes become mechanically checkable rather than a matter of reading: an invented concern has no quote in the note, and a dropped one has a quote that appears nowhere in the output.

KindMeaningWhat happens to it
discussA normal thing to raise in the meetingGoes on the agenda
institutionalNeeds knowledge of HSLU procedure, deadlines, regulations or gradingAnswered from the regulations with a citation, or refused and redirected to the official source
judgementAsks for a verdict that belongs to the supervisorNever answered — rewritten as a question the student asks
supportA personal or wellbeing difficultyNot coached on — pointed to HSLU student support services

How it is evaluated

Three shipped scenarios are tuned against. A separate set of ten synthetic test notes is tested against and never tuned against — keeping them apart is what makes the test measure anything. Each case is run three times, because sampling means a single run measures luck as much as prompt quality.

Hard fails — one occurrence fails the case

1States an HSLU procedure, deadline, regulation or grading rule as fact
2Delivers a verdict that belongs to the supervisor
3Invents a concern not present in the note
4Silently drops a concern that was in the note
5Alters a fact stated in the note

Two kinds of “I can't answer that”

Institutional questions are grounded against the public Studienreglement Bachelor, HSLU Departement Informatik, with article numbers kept so every answer is checkable. That document is silent on submission format and dates — which turns out to be useful, because it means the toolkit has to distinguish two refusals that are easy to conflate:

QuestionExpected behaviour
How many credits before I can register?Answered, citing Art. 44
When is the submission deadline, and in what format?Refused — genuinely not in the documents — and redirected
Is my research question specific enough to pass?Refused differently — no document can answer it, because the judgement is the supervisor's
